## TL;DR

- Ask why a three-dimensional CBCT dental scan is needed and how it will change your treatment plan.
- Discuss radiation, scan coverage, previous X-rays, pregnancy, and the clinic’s exposure settings.
- Confirm who reviews the images, whether you receive a written report, and how unexpected findings are explained.
- Compare the full fee, image access, reporting, consultation, and repeat-scan policy before booking.

A CBCT dental scan can give a dentist a three-dimensional view of the teeth, jaw, and surrounding structures. It still isn’t automatically the right test for every patient. Before choosing a listed clinic in Malaysia, ask the same practical questions at each clinic so you can compare the clinical reason, imaging process, records, and fee clearly. The [complete guide to finding and comparing a dental clinic](/guides/the-complete-guide-to-finding-and-comparing-a-dental-clinic) shows how to read a listing before you book.

## CBCT dental scan questions to ask before you book

### What should I ask the clinic before booking?

The clinic should explain why CBCT is being recommended and how the result will affect a diagnosis or treatment decision. The American Dental Association advises patients and dentists to discuss the need for dental X-rays according to individual clinical circumstances, rather than following a fixed schedule. A CBCT examination uses X-rays to create a 3D image, so the expected diagnostic benefit should justify the exposure.

Before contacting a clinic, prepare these details:

- Your previous dental images and referral notes.
- The treatment being considered and the area causing concern.
- Relevant medicines or medical conditions.
- Pregnancy or possible pregnancy information.

Save the questions on your phone. A clear answer at the booking stage often prevents confusion later.

## Why do I need a CBCT dental scan for this treatment?

### Why do I need a CBCT scan instead of a standard dental X-ray?

Ask which specific diagnosis or treatment decision requires a three-dimensional image. The FDA lists applications that can include implant planning, evaluation of abnormal or impacted teeth, jaw and facial assessment, [root canal](/services/root-canal) assessment, and dental trauma. That list does not mean every patient needs CBCT.

For example, someone considering a [dental implant](/services/dental-implants) can ask whether the scan is needed to assess the available bone, which area will be captured, and whether the result will change the proposed plan. If you already have a recent [dental X-ray](/services/dental-xrays), ask whether it answers the clinical question or whether CBCT would add information. Also ask what delay or alternative imaging would mean for your care. The treating dentist decides whether the scan is appropriate.

## What radiation questions should I ask the clinic?

### How does the clinic manage CBCT radiation?

Ask whether the clinic will use the smallest field of view and exposure setting that can answer the clinical question. The FDA recommends weighing the benefits of dental cone-beam CT against radiation risks and taking steps to reduce unnecessary exposure. The ADA also supports prescribing radiographs according to patient need and avoiding unnecessary repeat examinations.

Ask whether the clinic has reviewed your recent images before recommending another scan. Tell the dentist or imaging staff if you are pregnant or could be pregnant. They can explain the appropriate next step for your situation. Radiation questions should produce a specific explanation, not a blanket promise that every scan is risk-free.

## Which CBCT machine and scan settings will the clinic use?

### What should I know about the CBCT machine and scan settings?

Ask whether the scan will cover only the area needed for treatment or a wider region. The field of view affects the examination, so a patient being assessed for one tooth may need a different scan area from someone receiving a jaw evaluation.

You can also ask how the clinic manages movement, metal artefacts, and unclear images. Find out what happens if the first scan cannot answer the clinical question and whether a repeat examination would create another charge. Equipment models differ between clinics, so compare the clinic’s operating process, calibration arrangements, and image-quality checks rather than choosing by machine brand alone.

## Who reviews the images, and when will I receive the report?

### Who reads a CBCT scan and prepares the CBCT dental report?

Ask whether the treating dentist, an oral and maxillofacial radiologist, or another qualified clinician reviews the images. Then ask whether you receive a written CBCT dental report, a treatment explanation, or both. A report can be useful if you seek a second opinion or move your care to another clinic.

Ask how the clinic handles unexpected findings and whether you can access the original image files. A 2025 paper in [BMC Oral Health on dentists’ CBCT knowledge, attitudes, and practice](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-025-06870-x) provides research context, but its findings shouldn’t be treated as a rule for every clinic or dentist. Confirm the process directly with the listed clinic.

## What should I ask about preparation, safety, and comfort?

### How should I prepare for a CBCT dental scan?

Ask whether you need to remove glasses, jewellery, or removable dental appliances before the scan. Tell the clinic about pregnancy, recent imaging, medical conditions, and any difficulty standing or remaining still. These details can affect how staff prepare you and whether they need additional instructions.

A CBCT scan is generally non-invasive and usually requires you to remain still while the equipment captures images. The exact procedure and appointment process depend on the clinic. Follow the instructions provided rather than assuming preparation is identical everywhere. If you feel anxious, ask what will happen step by step before the scan begins.

## What will the CBCT dental scan cost, and what does the fee include?

### What does the CBCT scan price include?

Ask for the current CBCT dental scan cost before booking. Confirm whether the quoted fee includes image capture, image interpretation, a written report, consultation, and a copy of the files.

Two clinics can quote different amounts while offering different scan coverage or reporting arrangements. Ask whether a repeat scan would carry another charge and whether follow-up discussion is included. A lower CBCT scan price isn’t necessarily comparable if it excludes the report or consultation. Listed clinic information can help you compare providers, but confirm all current fees directly with the clinic.

## How should I compare clinics listed for CBCT dental scans?

### What should I check when comparing a CBCT dental clinic?

Choose the clinic that gives a clear clinical explanation and documents what you will receive. Price alone doesn’t tell you whether two scans offer the same coverage, review process, or records.

Send each shortlisted CBCT dental clinic the same questions and record the answers:

- Why is CBCT needed for this treatment decision?
- What area will the scan cover, and which settings will be used?
- How will the clinic limit unnecessary exposure or repeat imaging?
- Who reviews the images, and is a written report included?
- Can you receive the images and report for your records?
- What does the fee include, and what happens if the image is unclear?
- Who explains the findings and arranges follow-up care?

Use the directory to shortlist clinics by location, then contact them with this checklist. Comparing like with like gives you a better basis for booking than choosing the first quoted price.

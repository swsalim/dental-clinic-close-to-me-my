A dark mark after years of kopi or teh tarik may be colour sitting on the tooth surface. A change on one tooth, a tooth that has been damaged, or a mark that keeps changing needs a dentist to look at it. What you see in a mirror, or in a photograph, does not establish the cause.

This guide explains, in general terms, how a teeth stain differs from plaque and tartar, what daily cleaning can and cannot do, and how to find a clinic in Malaysia. It does not diagnose a mark on your tooth.

## What a teeth stain is

A teeth stain is a visible change in tooth colour. It may look yellow, brown, grey, white, or darker than the tooth beside it. Colour on the outer surface often relates to food, drinks, tobacco, or plaque. Colour from inside the tooth is a different finding and needs a professional assessment.

Plaque is a soft film. Tartar is plaque that has hardened. Tartar can look yellow or brown near the gum line, and it is a deposit stuck to the tooth, not a simple colour change. A toothbrush does not reliably scrape it off. [Plaque on teeth: how it differs from tartar](/guides/plaque-on-teeth) sets out that difference, including why a tea or coffee stain can look like calculus when it is not. Only an examination can tell them apart.

The American Dental Association’s whitening topic says whitening can be effective on both extrinsic and intrinsic staining, and that only natural teeth can be whitened, not tooth-coloured restorations. A crown, filling, or veneer can therefore stay a different colour from a tooth that has been whitened. Read the [ADA whitening guidance](https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/whitening) for that distinction. Whitening is not a way to find out why one tooth changed colour.

## Common causes of a teeth stain

Several causes can overlap. People who often drink kopi, teh tarik, or other strongly coloured drinks may notice gradual darkening, particularly where brushing misses the gum line or the spaces between teeth. That is a description of how surface colour collects. It is not a finding about any one person.

Cleveland Clinic lists smoking and other tobacco use, dental trauma, poor oral hygiene, and certain foods, drinks, and medications among contributors to tooth discolouration. It also says stains cling to dental plaque, so plaque left in place makes discolouration more likely. Its [tooth discolouration page](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/10958-tooth-discoloration) is general background.

Other everyday reasons people notice a difference:

- **Food and drinks.** Coffee, tea, and other strongly coloured items can leave deposits on the outer surface.
- **Tobacco.** Smoking and other tobacco use can contribute to yellow or brown colour.
- **Plaque and tartar.** The film can hold pigment, and a rough deposit collects more of it. See [plaque on teeth](/guides/plaque-on-teeth).
- **Ageing.** Teeth can look less bright as their structure changes over time.
- **Dental work or injury.** A filling, crown, or previously damaged tooth may not match the teeth beside it.

Do not scrape tartar off with a blade, pin, or other household tool. You can cut the gum or scratch the tooth surface. The same warning is on the plaque guide, because the deposit people try to chip off is often tartar rather than a loose stain.

## What home care can and cannot do

Home care works best against new surface deposits. It cannot promise that every teeth stain will disappear, especially when the colour is inside the tooth or on a restoration.

Brush twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste and clean between the teeth with floss or another method the dentist has said fits the gaps. Pay attention to the gum line and the back teeth. Rinsing with water after kopi or tea can reduce leftover residue. Spending less time sipping coloured drinks limits how often the surface is bathed in them. MouthHealthy’s advice on plaque is the same daily habit: brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste and clean between the teeth. The [plaque guide](/guides/plaque-on-teeth) explains why that removes soft film and does not remove hardened tartar.

The ADA says the most common unwanted effects of whitening a living tooth are temporary sensitivity and gum inflammation. Follow the product directions. Whitening only changes natural teeth. Ask a registered dentist before whitening if you have pain, bleeding, restorations, or you are not sure why the tooth looks different. If whitening has already made a tooth sensitive, the [sensitive teeth](/guides/sensitive-teeth) guide lists questions to take to the clinic. Stop and ask, rather than adding another product.

The NHS teeth-whitening page says whitening is something people consider when teeth have become stained or darker, including with age, drinks such as coffee and tea, or smoking. It also says whitening is not recommended if you have crowns or implants, because it can only lighten natural teeth.

| Situation | Practical next step |
| --- | --- |
| Light marks after coffee or tea | Keep brushing, clean between the teeth, and arrange a routine check-up |
| A hard yellow or brown deposit at the gum line | Ask a dentist whether a professional cleaning is suitable |
| One tooth changes colour or becomes sensitive | Book an assessment rather than starting whitening |

Home care is the wrong tool in these situations:

- Do not use a sharp tool to remove tartar.
- Do not treat pain or bleeding as a cosmetic problem.
- Do not assume a shop whitening product is suitable for crowns, fillings, or veneers.
- Do not delay an assessment for a stain that keeps changing.

## Scaling and whitening are different

Scaling is the professional removal of plaque and hardened tartar. A dentist or other dental professional examines the mouth first and decides what cleaning is suitable. Cleaning may make a surface look better when the colour was coming from deposits. It is not automatically a whitening procedure. It will not necessarily change colour inside a tooth, the shade of a filling, or the colour of a crown.

A person with brown buildup behind the lower front teeth may see a cleaner surface after tartar is removed. A grey area inside one tooth may remain and need its own assessment. Those are examples of why the two findings are not interchangeable. They are not a prediction for your mouth.

Home scraping is a poor substitute for professional instruments. It can cut the gums and leave scratches that collect more plaque. Ask the clinic whether the visit is a routine scale and clean or a gum assessment, and what the written estimate includes. Clinics that list routine cleaning are under [General Dentistry](/services/general-dentistry). Clinics that list gum treatment are under [Periodontics](/services/periodontics).

A dental dam is a sheet used during some procedures to isolate the tooth being treated and keep the area clearer. It is not a stain-removal treatment. If a clinic mentions one, ask which procedure it is for.

## When to see a dentist

Book an appointment if a stain is still there after normal cleaning, appears suddenly, spreads, affects one tooth, or looks different from the teeth around it. Arrange care sooner if you also have pain, sensitivity, bleeding gums, swelling, a chip, or a damaged tooth.

The NHS tooth-decay page says to see a dentist for toothache, a sharp pain in a tooth when eating or drinking hot, cold, or sweet things, or gums that are painful, swollen, or bleed when you brush. It also lists white, brown, or black spots as something you may see if a hole has formed. A spot is not, by itself, a diagnosis of decay. It is a reason to have the tooth examined. Read [Tooth decay](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/tooth-decay/) for that wording. Cleveland Clinic likewise says to arrange a dental visit promptly if discolouration comes with pain, bleeding gums, or a hole in a tooth.

The appointment may include a look at the teeth and gums and questions about drinks, tobacco, and previous dental work. A dental x-ray is not automatic for a stain. The dentist decides whether an image, or another test, would change the finding. Tell the clinic about symptoms, earlier treatment, and any whitening product you have used.

Seek urgent dental care for severe pain, facial swelling, or signs of a serious infection. If swelling makes breathing or swallowing difficult, call 999 or go to a hospital emergency department. Do not try to diagnose a dark mark from a photograph.

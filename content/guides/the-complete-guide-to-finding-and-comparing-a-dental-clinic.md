A dental clinic is a practice where a dentist examines teeth and gums and offers the services shown on that clinic's profile. On this directory you start from a place you can reach, open the profiles, and compare the same fields on each one: address, phone, hours, services, and the dentists listed there.

The pages below use the service names this site already publishes, including [General Dentistry](/services/general-dentistry), [Dental X-rays](/services/dental-xrays), [Periodontics](/services/periodontics), [Oral Surgery](/services/oral-surgery), [Root Canal Treatment](/services/root-canal), [Wisdom Tooth Extraction](/services/wisdom-tooth-extraction), and [Emergency Dentistry](/services/emergency-dentistry). Pick the service that matches the visit you are planning, then read the clinic profile before you book.

## What Dental Care Does a Clinic Usually Provide?

Dental care at a clinic is the set of services that clinic has chosen to list. A profile is a description of that practice, and two clinics on the same street can list different services. Read the services block on the profile and treat that list as the menu you can ask about. If a service is missing, ask the clinic whether they offer it or whether they refer patients elsewhere.

[General Dentistry](/services/general-dentistry) on this site covers routine checkups, cleaning, fillings, and basic dental care. That is the usual starting point when you want an examination and a plan, and you do not yet know which treatment you need. A first visit often begins with the dentist looking at your teeth and gums, asking what brought you in, and telling you what they can see. You can ask them to write down the findings and the next step.

Other listed services sit next to general care, and each one has its own page of clinics:

- [Cosmetic Dentistry](/services/cosmetic-dentistry) covers aesthetic treatments such as veneers, whitening, and smile makeovers.
- [Pediatric Dentistry](/services/pediatric-dentistry) is dental care for children and adolescents.
- [Orthodontics](/services/orthodontics) and [Braces](/services/braces) cover alignment. [Invisalign](/services/invisalign) is listed separately for clear aligners.
- [Periodontics](/services/periodontics) covers treatment of gum disease and the structures that support the teeth.
- [Endodontics](/services/endodontics) and [Root Canal Treatment](/services/root-canal) cover care of the dental pulp.
- [Prosthodontics](/services/prosthodontics) covers crowns, bridges, and dentures. [Dental Implants](/services/dental-implants) and [Veneers](/services/veneers) have their own pages.
- [Teeth Whitening](/services/teeth-whitening) is listed on its own.

For appliance types, quotations, aligners, and retainers, read [Braces Options Compared: Types, Costs, and Clinic Services](/guides/braces-options-compared).

Use those pages when you already know the category. Use a city page when you know the place and still need to see which clinics list the service. A clinic can appear on more than one service page if it has attached more than one service.

Dentists who practise in Malaysia are registered with the Malaysian Dental Council. A profile may show a dentist's name, bio, specialty, and qualification supplied for the listing. Those fields are not a registration check. Use [Check registration, contact details, and what a fee includes](#check-registration-contact-details-and-what-a-fee-includes) before you book.

Public dental services are run through the Ministry of Health Malaysia. Private clinics set their own appointments and fees. This directory lists clinics that have a profile here. It does not rank them, and a listing is a starting point for a phone call.

## When to Book a Dental Cleaning

Book a dental cleaning when you want a dentist to examine your teeth and remove the deposits they find, or when the clinic has asked you to return for a recall visit. A cleaning is part of the basic care described under [General Dentistry](/services/general-dentistry). Some clinics also list [Periodontics](/services/periodontics) when gum treatment is part of the visit. Check both labels on the profile so you know which page the clinic used.

People book a cleaning for practical reasons:

- The clinic gave you a recall date after the last examination.
- You can see deposits on the teeth, or your teeth feel rough along the gum line.
- Your gums bleed when you brush or floss, and you want a dentist to look at the cause.
- It has been a long time since anyone examined your mouth.
- You are about to start another treatment, such as braces or a crown, and the dentist wants the teeth cleaned first.

The right interval is the one the dentist gives you after looking at your mouth. Clinics do not all use the same recall. Ask what they saw, why they chose that interval, and what you should do if bleeding or roughness returns before the next date.

Say what you want when you call. "I would like a checkup and a cleaning" tells the receptionist which appointment to offer. Ask how long the visit is, whether the dentist examines you on the same day, and what you should bring. A government identification card is a common request at Malaysian clinics. Ask anyway, because the desk will tell you what that clinic needs.

Fees differ by clinic. A cleaning quote can change with the amount of deposit, whether gum treatment is included, whether an image is taken, and how many visits the dentist plans. Ask for the quote in writing. Ask what the fee includes and what would be billed separately. A figure from one clinic does not tell you what the next clinic will charge.

If the clinic you want is full, use the same city page and call the next profile that lists General Dentistry. In Kuala Lumpur, start at [Kuala Lumpur](/kuala-lumpur) or a neighbourhood such as [Bukit Bintang](/kuala-lumpur/bukit-bintang) or [Cheras](/kuala-lumpur/cheras). In Selangor, [Petaling Jaya](/selangor/petaling-jaya) and [Shah Alam](/selangor/shah-alam) are separate area pages. Keep the shortlist to clinics you can actually travel to.

## Why a Dental X Ray May Be Recommended

A dentist may recommend a dental x-ray when they need to see a part of the tooth or the bone that the mirror does not show. That includes the contact between two teeth, the area under an existing filling, and the tissue around a root. [Dental X-rays](/services/dental-xrays) on this directory means diagnostic imaging to examine dental and bone structures. Open that page if you want clinics that have attached the service to their profile.

You can ask three questions before you agree to an image:

1. What are you trying to see that the examination did not show?
2. Will the image change what you recommend today?
3. Is the image included in the written quote, or is it a separate fee?

Those questions keep the visit concrete. The dentist should be able to point to a tooth or an area and explain the reason. If you are pregnant, tell the dentist before any image is taken so they can decide how to proceed. If you have had recent images at another clinic, ask whether you can bring them. A second clinic may still want its own images. That is a question for the dentist who is treating you.

An image is a record of that visit. Ask for a copy if you want to take it to another clinic later. Ask what the file format is and how long the clinic keeps it. Practices store records in different ways, so the answer belongs to that clinic.

Do not treat a directory label as an order to have an x-ray. The label means the clinic listed imaging among its services. The decision sits with the dentist after an examination, and with you after you have heard the reason and the fee.

If a clinic profile lists Dental X-rays and also lists [Endodontics](/services/endodontics) or [Oral Surgery](/services/oral-surgery), the image may be part of planning that treatment. Ask the clinic to separate the imaging fee from the treatment fee in the quote so you can see both numbers before you book the longer visit.

## Dental Services to Compare on a Clinic Profile

Compare profiles on the same fields. A star rating is one field among several, and it does not describe the treatment you need. Read the profile in this order.

**Place.** Check the area and the street address. A clinic in [George Town](/penang/george-town) is a different trip from a clinic in [Butterworth](/penang/butterworth), even though both sit under [Penang](/penang). The same applies to [Johor Bahru](/johor/johor-bahru) and the rest of [Johor](/johor). Use the map on the profile to see the pin, then decide whether you can reach it at the time of day you plan to go.

**Phone and website.** The profile shows a phone number when the clinic has one on file. Some profiles also show a website. Call the phone number on the profile. Use the website if you want a second source for hours, and still confirm by phone. Listing hours can change.

**Hours.** Weekly hours, special hours, and a public-holiday flag can appear on a listing. Read the day you plan to visit. If the day is blank or the clinic is marked closed, pick another day or another clinic. Call before you leave. Reception can tell you whether the dentist you want is in, and whether the slot is long enough for a cleaning, an image, or a consultation.

**Services.** Match the services on the profile to the visit. A clinic that lists [Emergency Dentistry](/services/emergency-dentistry) is stating that urgent care is one of its services. A clinic that lists only [Teeth Whitening](/services/teeth-whitening) and [Veneers](/services/veneers) is describing a different kind of practice. Neither label tells you that the clinic can see you today. The phone call does.

**Dentists.** Open the dentist names linked from the clinic. Read the specialty and qualification fields when they are filled in. Then ask the clinic who will see you at the appointment you are booking. The dentist on the profile and the dentist on the day should be a question you ask, not an assumption.

**Description, photos, and reviews.** The about text, the gallery, and the reviews are part of the listing. Read them as descriptions from the clinic and from patients. They are useful for practical details such as parking notes or how the desk handles appointments. They are a poor substitute for an examination. [5 Dental Clinic Rating Details Worth Checking Before You Call](/guides/dental-clinic-ratings-checklist) walks through how to read those reviews before you phone.

**Closed clinics.** If a profile is marked permanently closed, skip it and return to the city page.

When you have two or three profiles, write a short comparison for yourself:

- Clinic name and area
- Service you need, copied from the profile
- Next available appointment, from the phone call
- Who the dentist will be
- Written quote, and what it includes
- Whether you can reach the clinic without rushing

Drop any clinic that cannot answer those points. Then book the one that fits the visit, the dentist, and the trip.

[Dentists](/dentists) is the directory of dentist profiles across Malaysia. Use it when you have a dentist's name and want their page. Use [Browse](/browse) when you are still choosing a state.

## Check registration, contact details, and what a fee includes

A directory profile is not a registration check. The Oral Health Programme, Ministry of Health Malaysia, states that the functions of the Malaysian Dental Council include registration of dental practitioners and maintenance of the Malaysian Dental Register. Ask the clinic for the full name of the dentist who will see you, then check that name on the Council's register. The programme's [Malaysian Dental Council](https://hq.moh.gov.my/ohp/en/component/content/article/9-malaysian-dental-council?Itemid=101&catid=2) page is the public description used here, and it points readers to the Council's website for further details. A star rating does not confirm registration. The name on a listing does not either.

Before you send personal or health information, confirm that the phone number, address, and booking route belong to that clinic. Use the details on the live profile, and confirm them when you speak to the clinic. Share only what the clinic needs to arrange the appointment. Do not upload dental records, photographs of your teeth, or a medical history through a form until you know who receives it. This directory does not examine you, and a form is not a consultation.

A price shown before the visit may be a starting fee for a consultation only. Do not agree to treatment from that figure alone. Ask whether it includes the examination, any X-rays, a cleaning, medicines, and a review visit. Ask for the answer in writing: what the expected fee is, what the consultation includes, and which items would be billed separately. Clinics set their own fees. This directory does not publish a fee schedule.

The cleaning and imaging sections on this page already ask you to name the procedure in a quote. This section is the check those quotes share. A lower starting price is not a reason to skip it.

## What Does Calculus Dental Treatment Involve?

Calculus is the hard deposit that forms when plaque stays on a tooth and hardens. People also call it tartar. You may feel it as a rough edge near the gum, or see it as a yellow or brown line. A dentist confirms what the deposit is during an examination. Home photos and a directory page cannot do that.

Treatment is the removal of those deposits as part of a cleaning or gum visit. Clinics often describe that visit as scaling. On this directory, look for [General Dentistry](/services/general-dentistry) for a routine cleaning and [Periodontics](/services/periodontics) when the clinic lists gum treatment. Ask the clinic which label matches the appointment they are offering you.

A typical conversation at the clinic covers four points:

1. Where the deposit is, and whether the gums are involved.
2. What the dentist proposes to remove at this visit.
3. Whether you need a second visit.
4. What the fee includes.

The dentist should tell you if the plan is limited to the deposits they can see, or if they also want to clean under the gum. Those are different appointments. Ask which one they are booking, how long it takes, and whether the teeth are likely to feel sensitive afterwards. Sensitivity is something to ask about. It is also something to report if it lasts longer than the dentist said it would.

Calculus can return if plaque is left in place. The dentist can show you the spots they want you to clean at home and the recall date they recommend. Follow the instructions they give you for your mouth. A general article cannot set that date for you.

If a clinic's quote for "cleaning" is vague, ask them to write "scaling" or the exact procedure name they use, the tooth areas involved, and the number of visits. Vague quotes are hard to compare. Two written quotes that name the same procedure are easier to put side by side, and you can still choose the clinic that is closer or that can see you sooner.

Bring a list of medicines you take to the visit, and tell the dentist about any medical condition they should know before they treat your gums. The dentist uses that information to plan the visit. This page cannot tell you which medicines matter for you.

## When Might Dental Surgery Be Needed?

Dental surgery is a procedure the dentist describes as surgical, after an examination. On this directory the related services are [Oral Surgery](/services/oral-surgery), [Wisdom Tooth Extraction](/services/wisdom-tooth-extraction), and [Dental Implants](/services/dental-implants). Oral Surgery is listed as surgical procedures such as extractions and dental implants. Wisdom Tooth Extraction is the removal of third molars when they are impacted or crowded. Dental Implants are described as replacements for missing teeth that use a titanium post.

A dentist may discuss surgery in situations like these:

- A tooth is too broken for a filling, and the dentist is considering removal.
- A wisdom tooth is stuck against the tooth in front of it, or it has not come through, and the dentist wants to talk about removal.
- A tooth is missing and you have asked about replacing it with an implant.
- Gum or root treatment has not been enough, and the dentist is referring you for a surgical option.

The dentist makes that judgement after looking at you and, when they need it, after an image. Ask them to explain the option they recommend, the option of leaving the tooth or replacing it another way, and what happens if you wait. [Prosthodontics](/services/prosthodontics) is the page for crowns, bridges, and dentures if a replacement other than an implant is part of the conversation. [Root Canal Treatment](/services/root-canal) is the page for saving a tooth by treating the pulp, which is a different plan from removing the tooth.

Before you book surgery, ask for a written quote and a written plan. The plan should name the tooth, the procedure, the type of anaesthetic they expect to use, the number of visits, and who will do the work. Ask whether a specialist will see you or whether the general dentist at that clinic will do it. Ask what the fee includes: the surgery, the images, the medicines, and the review visit. Ask what would cost more if the plan changes on the day.

Ask how you will get home if the clinic expects you to be drowsy, and ask what to do if bleeding is heavier than they described. For severe swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, trauma, or difficulty breathing or swallowing, go to a hospital emergency department or call 999. That instruction stands even if you already have a clinic appointment later in the week.

Surgery is a reason to confirm registration and the dentist's name again, using the register check above. Ask who is registered with the Malaysian Dental Council and who will be in the room. If the clinic refers you to another practice, ask for the name of that practice and check its profile, or ask them for the phone number and call it yourself.

Pick the clinic from a real place you can return to for the review visit. A surgical plan with two visits is a poor match for a clinic you cannot reach the second time. [Browse](/browse) lists the states and cities. Choose the area, open two profiles that list the surgical service, call both, and keep the written quote from the clinic you book.

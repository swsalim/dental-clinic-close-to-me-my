Plaque teeth is a common way to describe the film that collects along the gum line and between teeth. Plaque is soft. Tartar is the hard form of that film, and dentists also call tartar calculus. Knowing the difference helps you ask better questions. It does not tell you whether your gums or teeth need treatment. A registered dentist has to look in your mouth.

## What plaque on teeth is

MouthHealthy, the American Dental Association’s public site, describes plaque as a sticky film on the teeth that contains bacteria. After a meal or snack that contains sugar, those bacteria can release acids that attack enamel, the hard outer surface of a tooth. Repeated acid attacks can break enamel down and lead to cavities. The same film, left along the gums, is involved in gum disease.

Plaque is not food you can rinse away with water. It forms again through the day, including on teeth that look clean. A fuzzy feeling near the gums, especially behind the lower front teeth, is a common description. The film can look pale until food, drink, or tobacco stains it.

Orthodontic brackets, crowded teeth, and a dry mouth can make the film harder to disturb. That is a reason to ask a dentist for a method you can manage. Clinics that list examinations and cleaning sit under [General Dentistry](/services/general-dentistry).

## How plaque differs from tartar and calculus

Tartar and calculus are two names for the same hardened deposit. MouthHealthy says plaque that is not removed with thorough daily brushing and cleaning between the teeth can eventually harden into calculus or tartar. Once it hardens, it makes the teeth more difficult to keep clean. A toothbrush and floss are meant for soft plaque. They do not reliably scrape off a hard deposit stuck to the enamel or the root.

People often notice tartar as a hard, yellow or brown crust at the gum line, or as a rough edge the tongue keeps finding. A stain from tea, coffee, or tobacco can look similar and still be a surface stain rather than calculus. Only a dental examination can tell those apart. Do not try to chip a hard deposit off with a pin, a scaler bought online, or a fingernail. That can scratch the root or injure the gum.

When tartar collects above the gum line, MouthHealthy says the gum can become swollen and may bleed easily. That early stage of gum disease is called gingivitis. Deposits can also sit below the gum line, where you cannot see them. Bleeding when you brush is a reason to book a dentist. It is not, by itself, a diagnosis, and brushing harder is not the answer this page can give you.

Gum disease that goes beyond a simple cleaning is a different conversation. Clinics that list care for the gums and the tissues that support the teeth appear under [Periodontics](/services/periodontics). Ask a general dentist whether your visit is a routine scale and clean or whether they recommend a gum assessment or a referral.

## Signs people notice at home

None of these signs proves what is wrong. They are reasons to stop guessing and arrange an examination:

- a fuzzy coating on the teeth that returns quickly after brushing;
- gums that look puffy, dark red, or bleed when you brush or clean between the teeth;
- a hard yellow or brown edge at the gum line that brushing does not shift;
- a bad taste or ongoing bad breath that you cannot explain by a recent meal;
- teeth that look longer, or gums that seem to have pulled back;
- tooth pain, a hole, or sensitivity, which may be decay rather than plaque alone.

Decay and plaque are related, and they are not the same appointment question. If you are comparing clinics because of a cavity, use [Caries dental care: questions to ask before choosing a clinic](/guides/dental-caries-questions) alongside this page. Take photos of a listing if that helps you remember the clinic name. Do not use a photo of your own teeth as a diagnosis.

Swelling of the face, fever, pus, or trouble swallowing or breathing needs prompt care. Call a dental clinic and, if breathing is difficult, seek emergency medical help. Do not wait for a routine cleaning slot.

## Daily removal of soft plaque

MouthHealthy’s prevention list is practical: visit a dentist regularly, brush twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste, and clean between the teeth with floss every day. That advice is about removing soft plaque and lowering the chance of cavities. It is not a promise that you will never need a filling or a gum appointment.

A useful routine is specific. Ask the dentist to watch you brush and to show the spots you miss, often the gum line, the tongue side of the lower front teeth, and the back molars. Ask whether floss, an interdental brush, or another cleaner fits the spaces between your teeth. Wider gaps and tighter contacts are not cleaned the same way. If your hands are stiff, say so. The clinic can suggest a grip that you can actually use.

Fluoride toothpaste is the type MouthHealthy names for this daily habit. The amount, and any high-fluoride paste, is a decision for the dentist, especially for a child. Spit toothpaste out rather than swallowing it.

Frequent sugary snacks and sipping sweet drinks give plaque bacteria more chances to make acid. Ask the dentist which daily drink is worth changing first. Mention smoking or vaping in the medical history. A mouthwash or a home remedy is not a substitute for brushing and an examination. If you want to use one, ask whether it conflicts with your toothpaste or any treatment.

## When a professional scaling and cleaning is needed

A dental cleaning, often called scaling and polishing, is how a clinic removes tartar on the teeth and stains that home care cannot. You cannot see every deposit, so “my teeth feel smooth” is not a test you can rely on. The dentist decides whether a cleaning is due from the examination, your gum health, and how quickly calculus forms for you. There is no single interval that fits every patient in Malaysia, and this page will not invent one.

Book a dental visit for a cleaning assessment if you notice a hard deposit, bleeding gums, or it has been a long time since anyone examined your mouth. Also book if you are about to start braces or whitening, because those treatments are harder to manage when plaque and tartar are left in place. Ask the clinic:

- Is this a routine scale and clean, or do my gums need a deeper assessment?
- Will you examine for decay at the same visit?
- Do you expect more than one visit?
- What does the written estimate include, in Malaysian ringgit?
- What should I do at home afterwards, and when should I return?

A scale can leave teeth feeling sensitive for a short time, especially if tartar had covered the roots. Ask what is expected after the cleaning they performed, and who to call if pain is strong or swelling starts.

Gum disease, loose teeth, or deposits under the gum may need care beyond a routine polish. That is when a general dentist may treat you over several visits or refer you to a clinician who provides [periodontics](/services/periodontics). Ask what was found, what happens if you delay, and what the alternative is. A lower advertised cleaning fee is not useful if it excludes the examination you actually need.

## Questions to take to the clinic

Use the same list at each clinic so you can compare the answers:

1. What did you see: soft plaque, tartar, gum inflammation, decay, or something else?
2. Can I remove this at home, or does it need a professional scale?
3. Will you show me the areas I miss when I brush?
4. Is a [general dentistry](/services/general-dentistry) checkup enough, or do you recommend gum treatment?
5. What is included in the fee, and what would a second visit add?

Then find a clinic through the directory. Open [browse](/browse) and choose a state page such as [Kuala Lumpur](/kuala-lumpur), [Selangor](/selangor), [Penang](/penang), or [Johor](/johor). Read who the dentist is, call to confirm the cleaning visit, and ask for a written estimate before you agree to treatment.
